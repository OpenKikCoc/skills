# Writing rules

- Follow the accepted skeleton. For a multi-year plan the usual flow is goals,
  constraints, approach, workstreams, timeline, dependencies and risks; make
  priorities and tradeoffs visible. Other genres keep their own contract; this
  skill only adds the iteration layer.
- One concept card per named component before drafting: what it is, layer,
  general or scenario-specific, who owns the vocabulary, source. Unknown
  ownership stays unknown.
- Keep the owner's terminology. Do not coin jargon, translate component names,
  or simulate an artificial voice. Explain a term once at first use.
- Translate instruction keywords ("focus", "sharper") into reader-visible
  effects; ban the literal slogan in `lint.json` when it would leak into prose.
- Mark a proposal once, at the section lead or introducing sentence, not in
  every sentence. Mark again only for a new scope or to avoid confusing fact
  with proposal.
- State limits once, where they matter, rather than repeating the same
  disclaimer. Do keep required safety markers (blocked, read-only, missing
  authorization, stop or restore conditions).
- Each section adds a judgement. Merge repeated dependencies and repeated
  details instead of restating them.
- Figures: logic first. Every figure carries an argument and agrees with the
  sourced prose without reciting it. Build them with `web-diagram`; a figure
  claim without a source is deleted like any other claim.
- Measure style instead of asserting it: count first-person pronouns,
  parentheses, arrows against a sample the owner approved.
